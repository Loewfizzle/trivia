LOOPS - BLIZZARD

  Harsh, roaring mountain wind with clear whistling/howling tones on top of a dense roar. Very steady and alive. No voices/traffic. Peak -6 dBFS.
  Made as a loop: start and end match (-23 dB, same spectrum). The last-to-first sample step (0.068) is about 2x the largest step inside the file, so a faint tick at the seam is possible. A 5-10 ms crossfade in the engine removes it.
blizzard-loop-2.wav - 86.46 s, WAV 48k/24 stereo (klankbeeld, CC BY 4.0)
  Real blizzard over a suburban city (Den Bosch NL, 2017). Steady, full, hissing roar with gusts and wind whistling through the city. Very even level. Peak -7.5 dBFS.
  No voices/music. The tags mention "road", but no traffic is visible. There are two very brief faint tonal blips (~1.6 kHz at ~12 s and ~32 s), buried under the storm.
  Starts and ends abruptly (no fades; the first sample is non-zero). The seam step is within the normal range for the file, and start/end levels are similar (-28/-26 dB), so it loops acceptably. Use a short (50-200 ms) crossfade to be safe, and a few ms fade-in on the very first play.

winter-wind-loop-2.wav - 42.40 s, WAV 48k/32-bit FLOAT stereo (klankbeeld, CC BY 4.0)
  A real gale (up to 70 km/h) howling around a steel waterfront building in Vlissingen harbour (NL). Moderate, gusty, cold-sounding wind with a soft howl, and a strong low-frequency rumble component.
  No voices, traffic, waves or music seen. Peak -3 dBFS.
  Chosen over 97396 (jpnien), which has persistent narrow tonal lines (~400 Hz and several in 2-6 kHz) that sound like hum or whine and are not clearly city.
  ENDS: hard cut at both start and end (no fades; the first sample is about -0.15). Played once, it may click on start/stop, and the raw seam jump is large. Loop it with a 100-300 ms crossfade, and use a short fade-in/out when starting or stopping.
  32-bit float WAV plays in all modern browsers (Web Audio decodeAudioData). Some older tools prefer 16/24-bit.

RECOMMENDED USE
  - Blizzard scenes: use blizzard-loop-1 or -2 as the bed, alternating them so the storm doesn't feel repetitive. Layer sleet-glass-1 quietly for "inside by the window" moments.
  - Ordinary cold city night: use winter-wind-loop-1 (airy alley) or winter-wind-loop-2 (gusty, howling, more low end) at low volume under the music.
  - Random weather events: play a random gust every 8-20 s with random volume and pan.
      gust-1 / gust-5: big hero gusts.
      gust-2 / gust-4: outdoor mid gusts (fade out gust-2's end).
      gust-3 / gust-6: soft, distant or indoor gusts.
  - Tension/spooky moments: wind-whistle-1 (window whistle) or wind-whistle-2 (whistle between buildings) as one-shots over the bed.
  - Levels differ a lot (gust-3 is about -36 dBFS peak, winter-wind-loop-1 about -21). Set gain per file in the engine rather than editing the files.
